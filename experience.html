The one thing that matters

The reason a Superpower report feels the way it does isn't the data — it's that someone walked the member through their own body and made the findings feel authored. Matrix already has the raw material: the labs, the education library, the protocols, the goals, the intake answers, the bio-age, and Ava. What's missing is the choreography — the sequenced, narrated moments that turn a dashboard into a story about you.

Founder framing, restated. Everything in this blueprint serves it: turn assets we already shipped into a guided, felt journey.

The honest summary

Matrix today is a well-built set of rooms with no tour guide. A new member can walk into every room — dashboard, protocol, fitness, labs, Ava, education — and each room is competently furnished. But nothing greets them at the door, nothing explains why they're standing where they are, and once they leave, nothing calls them back. The product is strong at capability and weak at continuity. The gap the founder named — orientation — is the sharpest instance of a broader pattern: we render state; we don't narrate a journey.

What's genuinely good Have

Invite-gated entry, a real protocol/dose engine, a zero-judgment labs area, an operator-approved bio-age, and a live education library of monographs. The substance is here and it's not thin.

What's quietly broken Absent

The connective tissue. First-run orientation, proactive reach-out, lifecycle reminders, the "your results, explained" moment. The member is left to self-assemble meaning.

What's shipped but invisible Built-but-dark

An Ava context/aggregation surface with no consumer, a direct email adapter whose senders aren't rewired, and appointment/ICS scaffolding. We've paid for capability we're not spending. (Verify vs CX-1.)

Before designing anything new, spend what we already own. Each of these shipped and is either dark, un-consumed, or rendering data without narrating it. The cheapest experience wins live here.

Invite → loyal member every moment the product should own

The full arc. Each moment marked HANDLED, PARTIAL, or ABSENT by member-facing experience (operator-side handling doesn't count here). This is the map CX-1 will pin to file:line.

Invite issued Handled

Invite-gated access is real and verified on prod. Good. Opportunity: the invite itself could carry a first-person promise instead of a bare code.

Signup / account creation Handled

Onboarding V2 flow exists; signup completes and binds identity. Functional and clean.

Intake / questionnaire Partial

Intake is captured (operator has Intake Review). But the member's answers are never reflected back to them as "here's what you told us and here's what we heard." Intake is a form, not a mirror.

First screen after signup Absent

The named gap. Member lands on a dashboard with no orientation, no tour, no "start here." Every room is open and none is introduced. First impression = competent confusion.

Results ready / bio-age approved Absent

The emotional peak of the whole product. Today it surfaces a number and a lab table. No walkthrough, no meaning-making, no plan hand-off. This is where the guided tour must live.

First protocol assigned Partial

Protocol appears in a strong UI. But the "why this protocol, why these compounds, what it's for" narration isn't attached. The education to explain it exists — it's just not wired to the moment.

First dose Absent

The scariest action a member takes — often a first self-injection — happens with zero companionship. No walkthrough, no reassurance, no acknowledgement afterward.

First week Absent

The make-or-break window. No check-in, no "how's it going," no what-to-expect. The member is alone precisely when doubt peaks.

First lab upload Partial

Upload area exists and is well-designed. The "got it — here's what we see" acknowledgement and interpretation don't fire.

Adherence lapse Absent

Adherence is tracked. Nothing acts on a missed streak. A lapsing member is invisible to the product until they're gone.

Re-order / refill Partial (operator-side)

Fulfillment + refill stages exist in the operator/Airtable workflow. The member-facing "time to refill" nudge and one-tap reorder are not surfaced.

Ongoing check-in / milestone Absent

No cadence of "you're 30 days in, here's your progress." No milestone recognition. Momentum is never reflected back.

Churn risk / dormancy Absent

No signal model for "this member is drifting," no re-engagement play. Churn is discovered, not prevented.

Return / win-back Absent

A dormant member who comes back is greeted identically to an active one. No "welcome back, here's what changed." Re-entry is a cold start.

Tally (member-facing): 2 Handled · 4 Partial · 8 Absent across 14 moments. The front door is solid; the middle and the return are wide open. Note how many Absents are lifecycle (reach-out) rather than screen (build) problems — which is why channels and choreography, not new pages, are the leverage.

The guided tour post-approval, Superpower-style — the founder's ask, designed

Triggered the instant bio-age / results are operator-approved — the emotional peak we currently waste on a bare number. A page-by-page, narrated walkthrough of the member's own body and plan. Every step is fed by data we already have. Nothing here needs new capture — it needs sequencing and a voice.

1

"Here's you, today."

Open on the bio-age and the one-line headline. Warm, plain, human. Set the frame: this is a starting line, not a verdict.

"Your biological age came back at 38. You're 44. That four-year head start is real — and it's yours to grow."
Uses: bio-age (approved), member DOB/profile.
2

"What we measured, and what it means for you."

Walk the lab panels one at a time — not a table, a story. Each panel gets a plain-language read and a "why it matters to your goals" line. This is the Superpower feeling, exactly.

Uses: labs (0048 area), education monographs, Ava context surface, intake goals.
3

"You told us what you wanted. Here's how this maps to it."

Reflect the member's own intake goals back, then draw the line from each goal to a specific marker and protocol choice. Intake stops being a form and becomes the thread.

Uses: intake answers, goals, protocol assignment.
4

"Your protocol — and why each piece is here."

Introduce the protocol compound by compound: what it is, why it was chosen for you, what to expect. The education library supplies the substance; the tour supplies the sequencing.

Uses: protocol/dose engine, compound detail, education monographs.
5

"Your first dose — let's do it together."

A gentle first-dose walkthrough for the scariest step. Reconstitution/measurement handoff where relevant, reassurance, and a real acknowledgement after: you did the hard part.

Uses: dose calculator, protocol instructions, education.
6

"Here's the plan, and here's me checking in."

Close on the 30/60/90 shape and set the expectation of a relationship: what happens next week, what to watch, when the next lab is. Hand off to Ava as the ongoing companion — the tour's last act is introducing the guide who stays.

Uses: Ava context surface, appointment/ICS scaffolding, email adapter for the follow-up cadence.

What the tour needs

Already have Have

Bio-age, labs, protocols, compound detail, dose calculator, intake/goals, 18 education monographs, and the idle Ava context surface to assemble it all. The pantry is stocked.

Need to build New

The tour runner (a sequenced, narrated step UI), the narration layer (Ava-authored per-panel/per-compound reads), and the approval → tour trigger. Modest, high-leverage builds.

The unlock

Fire it on the bio-age approval event that already exists. Same trigger, transformed payload. This is the single highest impact-to-effort move in the product.

Guardrail. Every narrated read must respect the constitution: founder/operator-approved framing only, no represented licensure, zero-judgment tone preserved (INV / §3.4). The tour presents approved conclusions warmly — it never generates clinical claims on the fly.

The build path activation before construction

Sequenced so the member feels a difference at the end of every phase, and so each phase spends assets we already own before building new ones.

Phase 0 — Certify the ground days

Reconcile this blueprint with CX-1's forensic audit. Confirm real state of: email senders, Ava context consumer, notification tables, ICS surface, push/SMS. Turn every "verify vs CX-1" into a fact. No member-facing change — this de-risks everything after.

Phase 1 — The Tour highest leverage

Build the post-approval guided walkthrough (6 steps), fed by existing bio-age + labs + protocol + intake + education, narrated through the idle Ava context surface. Fire on the approval event that already exists. This is the founder's ask and the single biggest felt win. Ships the moment that currently wastes the emotional peak.

Phase 2 — Wake the channel activation

Rewire the direct email adapter and ship the 7 starter messages with triggers + suppression. Now the product reaches out. Welcome, results-ready, first-week, refill, milestone. The lifecycle stops being silent.

Phase 3 — Proactive Ava the companion

Give the Ava context surface a proactive consumer: Ava initiates at the key moments (labs in, day 7, day 30, drifting). One voice across tour, email, and chat. The system becomes a relationship.

Phase 4 — Close the loop retention

Adherence-lapse nudge, member-facing refill/reorder, milestone cadence, and a dormancy → win-back play. Own the middle and the return — the eight Absent moments on the lifecycle map.

Needs Jack — decisions this blueprint waits on

  • 1 · Approve the thesis Choreography over new capability. If yes, Phase 1 (the tour) is the next build lane.
  • 2 · Tour trigger = bio-age approval? Confirm the approval event is the right fire point, or name a different one.
  • 3 · Give Ava a voice for narration The tour's per-panel reads need an approved authoring boundary — what Ava may say vs. only present.
  • 4 · Green-light waking the email channel Rewiring senders is member-facing; it needs your go (and a from-address / domain confirm).
